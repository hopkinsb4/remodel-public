Design Palette

93 Waterman

1,220 sq ft · 2 bed / 2.5 bath · Irvine, CA

A father with three kids (9, 12, 21) and a cat, making a 1,220 sq ft, 2-bedroom house work for a family of five in the house they love. Two design drivers shaped everything: cat-friendly cleanliness (carpet and laminate flooring replaced with tile throughout, for real day-to-day maintainability) and genuine sleeping/privacy for four full-size humans in a home that was never built for that. A black-and-white checkerboard floor, set on the diagonal, runs through the entire home — bedrooms, bathrooms, living room, kitchen, dining room, entryways, closets — at one consistent size throughout. The original secondary bedroom becomes the primary suite (standalone furniture) in both scenarios below. What differs is how the family gets a third bedroom: Bunk Suite converts the original primary bedroom into built-in 3-berth bunks; Bump-Out keeps that room as simple twin-bed furniture and adds a new standalone bedroom off the dining room instead. All walls throughout are finished in glossy white-gray paint for easy wipe-down durability; matte black hardware and white painted drywall ceilings round out the shower material story.

Whole-house palette (flooring, paint, hardware) is identical in both scenarios. Toggle above to see how the third bedroom, its bath, and the powder room change.

A note on flexibility. The one must-have across both scenarios is the checkerboard tile throughout. Everything else — colors, materials, fixtures, and how to get the best value — is genuinely open to the design firm's judgment. This palette is a starting reference, not a locked spec. The general aesthetic direction we're after: fresh and current, the kind of space that photographs well today, but grounded enough in timeless choices that it won't read as dated in five or ten years.
